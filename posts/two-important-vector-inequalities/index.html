Two Important Vector Inequalities

3 Sep, 2026
| Edit page

Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

Proposition 1

For any x,y∈Rn\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^nx,y∈Rn,

∣x⋅y∣≤∥x∥ ∥y∥.|\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}|\leq \|\mathbf{x}\|\,\|\mathbf{y}\|.∣x⋅y∣≤∥x∥∥y∥.

Proof

If either vector is zero, both sides are zero and the inequality holds. Otherwise, define the unit vectors

a=x∥x∥andb=y∥y∥.\mathbf{a}=\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\|\mathbf{x}\|} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathbf{b}=\frac{\mathbf{y}}{\|\mathbf{y}\|}.a=∥x∥x​andb=∥y∥y​.

Because squared norms are nonnegative,

∥a+b∥2=∥a∥2+2a⋅b+∥b∥2=2+2a⋅b≥0,\begin{aligned} \|\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}\|^2 &=\|\mathbf{a}\|^2+2\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}+\|\mathbf{b}\|^2\\ &=2+2\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}\geq 0, \end{aligned}∥a+b∥2​=∥a∥2+2a⋅b+∥b∥2=2+2a⋅b≥0,​

so a⋅b≥−1\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}\geq -1a⋅b≥−1. Similarly,

∥a−b∥2=2−2a⋅b≥0,\|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\|^2 =2-2\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}\geq 0,∥a−b∥2=2−2a⋅b≥0,

which gives a⋅b≤1\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}\leq 1a⋅b≤1. Therefore,

−1≤a⋅b≤1.-1\leq \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}\leq 1.−1≤a⋅b≤1.

Substituting the definitions of a\mathbf{a}a and b\mathbf{b}b,

∣x⋅y∥x∥ ∥y∥∣≤1,\left| \frac{\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}} {\|\mathbf{x}\|\,\|\mathbf{y}\|} \right|\leq 1,​∥x∥∥y∥x⋅y​​≤1,

and hence

∣x⋅y∣≤∥x∥ ∥y∥.□|\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}|\leq \|\mathbf{x}\|\,\|\mathbf{y}\|. \tag*{$\square$}∣x⋅y∣≤∥x∥∥y∥.□

Triangle inequality

Proposition 2

For any x,y∈Rn\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^nx,y∈Rn,

∥x+y∥≤∥x∥+∥y∥.\|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}\|\leq \|\mathbf{x}\|+\|\mathbf{y}\|.∥x+y∥≤∥x∥+∥y∥.

Proof

Expanding the squared norm and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

∥x+y∥2=(x+y)⋅(x+y)=∥x∥2+∥y∥2+2x⋅y≤∥x∥2+∥y∥2+2∥x∥ ∥y∥=(∥x∥+∥y∥)2.\begin{aligned} \|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}\|^2 &=(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})\cdot(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})\\ &=\|\mathbf{x}\|^2+\|\mathbf{y}\|^2+2\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\\ &\leq \|\mathbf{x}\|^2+\|\mathbf{y}\|^2 +2\|\mathbf{x}\|\,\|\mathbf{y}\|\\ &=(\|\mathbf{x}\|+\|\mathbf{y}\|)^2. \end{aligned}∥x+y∥2​=(x+y)⋅(x+y)=∥x∥2+∥y∥2+2x⋅y≤∥x∥2+∥y∥2+2∥x∥∥y∥=(∥x∥+∥y∥)2.​

Both sides are nonnegative, so taking square roots gives

∥x+y∥≤∥x∥+∥y∥.□\|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}\|\leq \|\mathbf{x}\|+\|\mathbf{y}\|. \tag*{$\square$}∥x+y∥≤∥x∥+∥y∥.□
