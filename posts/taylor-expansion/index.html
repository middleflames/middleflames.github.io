Background

Suppose we have a real- or complex-valued function f(x)f(x)f(x) and want to approximate it near a point x0x_0x0​ with a simpler function. What should that approximation look like?

Call the approximation P(x)P(x)P(x). If P(x)P(x)P(x) is similar to f(x)f(x)f(x) near x0x_0x0​, it should at least have the same value there:

P(x0)=f(x0).P(x_0)=f(x_0).P(x0​)=f(x0​).

If fff has derivatives up to order nnn, we can make the approximation more accurate near x0x_0x0​ by matching those derivatives as well. A natural choice is an nnnth-degree polynomial:

Pn(x)=∑k=0nak(x−x0)k=a0+a1(x−x0)+a2(x−x0)2+⋯+an(x−x0)n.P_n(x)=\sum_{k=0}^n a_k(x-x_0)^k =a_0+a_1(x-x_0)+a_2(x-x_0)^2+\cdots+a_n(x-x_0)^n.Pn​(x)=k=0∑n​ak​(x−x0​)k=a0​+a1​(x−x0​)+a2​(x−x0​)2+⋯+an​(x−x0​)n.

We require

Pn(x0)=f(x0),Pn′(x0)=f′(x0),Pn′′(x0)=f′′(x0), ⋮Pn(n)(x0)=f(n)(x0).\begin{aligned} P_n(x_0)&=f(x_0),\\ P_n'(x_0)&=f'(x_0),\\ P_n''(x_0)&=f''(x_0),\\ &\ \vdots\\ P_n^{(n)}(x_0)&=f^{(n)}(x_0). \end{aligned}Pn​(x0​)Pn′​(x0​)Pn′′​(x0​)Pn(n)​(x0​)​=f(x0​),=f′(x0​),=f′′(x0​), ⋮=f(n)(x0​).​

Evaluating the derivatives of PnP_nPn​ at x0x_0x0​ gives

a0=f(x0)0!,a1=f′(x0)1!,a2=f′′(x0)2!, ⋮an=f(n)(x0)n!.\begin{aligned} a_0&=\frac{f(x_0)}{0!},\\ a_1&=\frac{f'(x_0)}{1!},\\ a_2&=\frac{f''(x_0)}{2!},\\ &\ \vdots\\ a_n&=\frac{f^{(n)}(x_0)}{n!}. \end{aligned}a0​a1​a2​an​​=0!f(x0​)​,=1!f′(x0​)​,=2!f′′(x0​)​, ⋮=n!f(n)(x0​)​.​

Therefore, the degree-nnn Taylor polynomial of fff about x0x_0x0​ is

Pn(x)=∑k=0nf(k)(x0)k!(x−x0)k.P_n(x)=\sum_{k=0}^n \frac{f^{(k)}(x_0)}{k!}(x-x_0)^k.Pn​(x)=k=0∑n​k!f(k)(x0​)​(x−x0​)k.

Taylor series

If fff has derivatives of every order at x0x_0x0​, its Taylor series about x0x_0x0​ is

∑n=0∞f(n)(x0)n!(x−x0)n.\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f^{(n)}(x_0)}{n!}(x-x_0)^n.n=0∑∞​n!f(n)(x0​)​(x−x0​)n.

When this series converges to f(x)f(x)f(x), we can write

f(x)=∑n=0∞f(n)(x0)n!(x−x0)n.f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f^{(n)}(x_0)}{n!}(x-x_0)^n.f(x)=n=0∑∞​n!f(n)(x0​)​(x−x0​)n.
