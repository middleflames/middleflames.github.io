Preliminary

Conditional Expectation

Definition

Let A,BA,BA,B be two r.v.s. The conditional expectation is defined as

E[B∣A]=∫Bb⋅p(b∣a).\mathbb{E}\left[B|A\right]=\int_B b \cdot p(b|a).E[B∣A]=∫B​b⋅p(b∣a).

Properties

Lemma 1

E[E[A∣B]]=E[A]\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[A|B]]=\mathbb{E}[A]E[E[A∣B]]=E[A].

Proof

E[E[A∣B]]=∫BP(B)∫Aa⋅P(A∣B)=∫B∫Aa⋅P(A∣B)P(B)=∫B∫Aa⋅P(A,B)=∫Aa∫BP(A,B)=∫Aa⋅P(A)=E[A].\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[A|B]] &= \int_B P(B)\int_A a \cdot P(A|B) \\ &= \int_B\int_A a\cdot P(A|B) P(B) \\ &= \int_B \int_A a \cdot P(A,B) \\ &= \int_Aa\int_B P(A,B) \\ &= \int_Aa\cdot P(A) \\ &= \mathbb{E}[A]. \end{aligned}E[E[A∣B]]​=∫B​P(B)∫A​a⋅P(A∣B)=∫B​∫A​a⋅P(A∣B)P(B)=∫B​∫A​a⋅P(A,B)=∫A​a∫B​P(A,B)=∫A​a⋅P(A)=E[A].​

■

Lemma 2

EA,B[f(A,B)]=EA[EB[f(A,B)∣A]]\mathbb{E}_{A,B}\left[f(A,B)\right] = \mathbb{E}_A \left[ \mathbb{E}_B \left[f(A,B)|A \right] \right]EA,B​[f(A,B)]=EA​[EB​[f(A,B)∣A]]

Proof

EA,B[f(A,B)]=∫A,BP(A,B)f(A,B)=∫A∫BP(B∣A)P(A)f(A,B)=∫AP(A)∫BP(B∣A)f(A,B)=∫AP(A)EB[f(A,B)∣A]=EA[EB[f(A,B)∣A]]..\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}_{A,B}[f(A,B)] &= \int_{A,B} P(A,B)f(A,B) \\ &= \int_A \int_B P(B\mid A)P(A)f(A,B) \\ &= \int_A P(A)\int_B P(B\mid A)f(A,B) \\ &= \int_A P(A)\mathbb{E}_B[f(A,B)\mid A] \\ &= \mathbb{E}_A\left[\mathbb{E}_B[f(A,B)\mid A]\right]. \end{aligned}.EA,B​[f(A,B)]​=∫A,B​P(A,B)f(A,B)=∫A​∫B​P(B∣A)P(A)f(A,B)=∫A​P(A)∫B​P(B∣A)f(A,B)=∫A​P(A)EB​[f(A,B)∣A]=EA​[EB​[f(A,B)∣A]].​.

■

Lemma 3

E[f(Y)X∣Y]=f(Y)E[X∣Y]\mathbb E[f(Y)X\mid Y] = f(Y)\mathbb E[X\mid Y]E[f(Y)X∣Y]=f(Y)E[X∣Y].

Bias-Variance Tradeoff of REINFORCE

Recall the REINFORCE algorithm:

∇θJ(πθ)=Eπ[(∑t=1H∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st))(∑t=1Hγt−1rt)].(1)\nabla_{\theta} J(\pi_{\theta}) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi} \left[ \left( \sum_{t=1}^{H} \nabla_{\theta} \log \pi_{\theta}(a_t \mid s_t) \right) \left( \sum_{t=1}^{H} \gamma^{t-1} r_t \right) \right]. \tag{1}∇θ​J(πθ​)=Eπ​[(t=1∑H​∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​))(t=1∑H​γt−1rt​)].(1)

When we use Monte-Carlo (sampling real trajectories) to estimate this expectation, it is an unbiased estimation as there is no systematic guess about system. However, the consequence of Monte-Carlo is high-variance, despite the constraint of discount factor γ\gammaγ.

Proposition 1

The REINFORCE algorithm provides an unbiased estimator of the policy gradient ∇θJ(πθ)\nabla_{\theta} J(\pi_{\theta})∇θ​J(πθ​).

Proof

For NNN independently sampled trajectories {τi}i=1N\{\tau_i\}_{i=1}^N{τi​}i=1N​, the Monte-Carlo estimator is

g^N=1N∑i=1N[(∑t=1H∇θlog⁡πθ(at(i)∣st(i)))(∑t=1Hγt−1rt(i))]. \widehat{g}_N = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \left[ \left( \sum_{t=1}^{H} \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t^{(i)}\mid s_t^{(i)}) \right) \left( \sum_{t=1}^{H}\gamma^{t-1}r_t^{(i)} \right) \right].g​N​=N1​i=1∑N​[(t=1∑H​∇θ​logπθ​(at(i)​∣st(i)​))(t=1∑H​γt−1rt(i)​)].

By the linearity of expectation,

E[g^N]=1N∑i=1N∇θJ(πθ)=∇θJ(πθ). \begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}[\widehat{g}_N] &=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla_\theta J(\pi_\theta)\\ &=\nabla_\theta J(\pi_\theta). \end{aligned}E[g​N​]​=N1​i=1∑N​∇θ​J(πθ​)=∇θ​J(πθ​).​

Therefore, the REINFORCE estimator is unbiased.

■

For the single-trajectory gradient estimator, we denote

g^(τ)=(∑t=1H∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st))⏟S(τ)(∑t=1Hγt−1rt)⏟R(τ). \hat{g}(\tau) = \underbrace{\left(\sum_{t=1}^{H}\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(a_t\mid s_t)\right)}_{S(\tau)} \underbrace{\left(\sum_{t=1}^{H}\gamma^{t-1}r_t\right)}_{R(\tau)}.g^​(τ)=S(τ)(t=1∑H​∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​))​​R(τ)(t=1∑H​γt−1rt​)​​.

Since the trajectories are independent,

Var⁡[g^N]=1NVar⁡[g^]. \operatorname{Var}[\hat g_N] = \frac{1}{N}\operatorname{Var}[\hat g].Var[g^​N​]=N1​Var[g^​].

Therefore,

Var⁡[g^N]=1N[Eτ∼πθ[R(τ)2∥S(τ)∥22]−∥∇θJ(πθ)∥22]. \operatorname{Var}[\hat g_N] = \frac{1}{N} \left[ \mathbb E_{\tau\sim\pi_\theta} \left[ R(\tau)^2\|S(\tau)\|_2^2 \right] - \|\nabla_\theta J(\pi_\theta)\|_2^2 \right].Var[g^​N​]=N1​[Eτ∼πθ​​[R(τ)2∥S(τ)∥22​]−∥∇θ​J(πθ​)∥22​].

From above, we could observe that the variance is dependent on two factors:

  1. The stochastic policy score S(τ)S(\tau)S(τ).
  2. Stochastic cumulative rewards.

REINFORCE: Reward-to-go

Recall the reward term in Eq. (1), for each action ata_tat​, it has to multiply the whole reward sequence from t=1t=1t=1 to HHH. However, the action at time ttt should only affect rewards from ttt onward. If we could remove the reward before ttt, without paying the price for estimation of the expectation, we could reduce the variance.

Proposition 2

∀t:Eπ[(∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st))(∑t′=1t−1γt′−1rt′)]=0\forall t: \mathbb{E}_\pi \left[ \left( \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t) \right) \left( \sum_{t^\prime=1}^{t-1}\gamma^{t^\prime-1}r_{t^\prime} \right) \right] = 0∀t:Eπ​[(∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​))(t′=1∑t−1​γt′−1rt′​)]=0

Proof

Eπ[∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st)(∑t′=1t−1γt′−1rt′)]=Eπ[Eat[∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st)(∑t′=1t−1γt′−1rt′)  |  s1,a1,r1,…,at−1,rt−1,st]](Lemma 1)=Eπ[(∑t′=1t−1γt′−1rt′)Eat∼πθ(⋅∣st)[∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st)]].(Lemma 3)\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}_\pi \left[ \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t) \left(\sum_{t'=1}^{t-1}\gamma^{t'-1}r_{t'}\right) \right]\\[6pt] &= \mathbb{E}_\pi \left[ \mathbb{E}_{a_t}\left[ \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t) \left(\sum_{t'=1}^{t-1}\gamma^{t'-1}r_{t'}\right) \;\middle|\; s_1,a_1,r_1,\ldots,a_{t-1},r_{t-1},s_t \right] \right] \quad\text{(Lemma 1)}\\[6pt] &= \mathbb{E}_\pi \left[ \left(\sum_{t'=1}^{t-1}\gamma^{t'-1}r_{t'}\right) \mathbb{E}_{a_t\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s_t)} \left[ \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t) \right] \right]. \quad\text{(Lemma 3)} \end{aligned}​Eπ​[∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​)(t′=1∑t−1​γt′−1rt′​)]=Eπ​[Eat​​[∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​)(t′=1∑t−1​γt′−1rt′​)​s1​,a1​,r1​,…,at−1​,rt−1​,st​]](Lemma 1)=Eπ​[(t′=1∑t−1​γt′−1rt′​)Eat​∼πθ​(⋅∣st​)​[∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​)]].(Lemma 3)​

where

Eat∼πθ(⋅|st)[(∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st))]=∑atπθ(at|st)∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st)=∑atπθ(at|st)∇θπθ(at∣st)πθ(at|st)(log-trick)=∇θ∑atπθ(at|st)=∇θ1=0.\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}_{a_t \sim \pi_\theta\left(\cdot \middle | s_t\right)}\left[\left( \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t) \right)\right] &= \sum_{a_t}\pi_\theta\left(a_t \middle | s_t\right) \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta\left(a_t\mid s_t\right) \\ &= \sum_{a_t}\pi_\theta\left(a_t \middle | s_t\right) \frac{\nabla_\theta\pi_\theta\left(a_t\mid s_t\right)}{\pi_\theta\left(a_t \middle | s_t\right)} \quad\text{(log-trick)}\\ &= \nabla_\theta\sum_{a_t}\pi_\theta\left(a_t \middle | s_t\right) \\ &= \nabla_\theta 1 = 0. \end{aligned}Eat​∼πθ​(⋅∣st​)​[(∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​))]​=at​∑​πθ​(at​∣st​)∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​)=at​∑​πθ​(at​∣st​)πθ​(at​∣st​)∇θ​πθ​(at​∣st​)​(log-trick)=∇θ​at​∑​πθ​(at​∣st​)=∇θ​1=0.​

Thus,

∀t:Eπ[(∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st))(∑t′=1t−1γt′−1rt′)]=0\forall t: \mathbb{E}_\pi \left[ \left( \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t) \right) \left( \sum_{t^\prime=1}^{t-1}\gamma^{t^\prime-1}r_{t^\prime} \right) \right] = 0∀t:Eπ​[(∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​))(t′=1∑t−1​γt′−1rt′​)]=0

■

Split the reward in Eq. (1), we get

∇θJ(πθ)=Eπ[∑t=1H∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st)(∑t′=1Hγt′−1rt′)]=∑t=1HEπ[∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st)(∑t′=1t−1γt′−1rt′+∑t′=tHγt′−1rt′)]. \begin{aligned} \nabla_\theta J(\pi_\theta) &= \mathbb{E}_\pi \left[ \sum_{t=1}^{H} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t) \left(\sum_{t'=1}^{H}\gamma^{t'-1}r_{t'}\right) \right]\\[6pt] &= \sum_{t=1}^{H}\mathbb{E}_\pi \left[ \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t) \left( \sum_{t'=1}^{t-1}\gamma^{t'-1}r_{t'} + \sum_{t'=t}^{H}\gamma^{t'-1}r_{t'} \right) \right]. \end{aligned}∇θ​J(πθ​)​=Eπ​[t=1∑H​∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​)(t′=1∑H​γt′−1rt′​)]=t=1∑H​Eπ​[∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​)(t′=1∑t−1​γt′−1rt′​+t′=t∑H​γt′−1rt′​)].​

Given Proposition 2, we can now have the reward-to-go estimation

∇θJ(πθ)=∑t=1HEπ[∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st)(∑t′=tHγt′−1rt′)].(2)\begin{aligned} \nabla_\theta J(\pi_\theta) &= \sum_{t=1}^{H}\mathbb{E}_\pi \left[ \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t) \left( \sum_{t'=t}^{H}\gamma^{t'-1}r_{t'} \right) \right]. \end{aligned} \tag{2}∇θ​J(πθ​)​=t=1∑H​Eπ​[∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​)(t′=t∑H​γt′−1rt′​)].​(2)

Proposition 2 guarantees the unbiasedness of Eq. (2), removing the past scores often could also reduce the variance.

Baseline Function

During the proof of Proposition 2, we know

Eat∼πθ(⋅|st)[(∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st))]=0.\mathbb{E}_{a_t \sim \pi_\theta\left(\cdot \middle | s_t\right)}\left[\left( \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t) \right)\right] =0.Eat​∼πθ​(⋅∣st​)​[(∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​))]=0.

For any function b(st)b(s_t)b(st​) independent of ata_tat​ given sts_tst​, we have

Eat∼πθ(⋅|st)[(∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st))b(st)]=0.\mathbb{E}_{a_t \sim \pi_\theta\left(\cdot \middle | s_t\right)}\left[\left( \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t) \right)b(s_t)\right] =0.Eat​∼πθ​(⋅∣st​)​[(∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​))b(st​)]=0.

In practice, we call such a function, which only relates to the state, a baseline function. Since removing the baseline from the estimation also guarantees the unbiasedness, we can subtract the baseline to concentrate the numerics, thus possibly to further reduce the variance.

Actor-Critic

Recall that QπQ^\piQπ is defined as

Qtπ(s,a)=Eπ[∑t′=tHγt′−trt′  |  st=s, at=a].{ Q_t^\pi(s,a) = \mathbb E_\pi\left[ \sum_{t'=t}^{H}\gamma^{t'-t}r_{t'} \;\middle|\; s_t=s,\ a_t=a \right] }.Qtπ​(s,a)=Eπ​[t′=t∑H​γt′−trt′​​st​=s, at​=a].

Observing Eq. (2), we can use Lemma 1 again to separate at,sta_t,s_tat​,st​ as follows:

∇θJ(πθ)=∑t=1HEπ[∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st)∑t′=tHγt′−1rt′]=∑t=1HEst,at[Eπ[∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st)∑t′=tHγt′−1rt′  ∣  st,at]](lemma 1)=∑t=1HEst,at[∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st)Eπ[∑t′=tHγt′−1rt′  |  st,at]].(lemma 3)=∑t=1HEπ[γt−1∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st)Qtπθ(st,at)]=∑t=1HEπ[γt−1∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st)(Qtπθ(st,at)−b(st))].(baseline function) \begin{aligned} \nabla_\theta J(\pi_\theta) &= \sum_{t=1}^{H} \mathbb E_\pi\left[ \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t) \sum_{t'=t}^{H}\gamma^{t'-1}r_{t'} \right] \\[6pt] &= \sum_{t=1}^{H}\mathbb E_{s_t,a_t}\Bigg[ \mathbb E_\pi\Bigg[ \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t) \sum_{t'=t}^{H}\gamma^{t'-1}r_{t'} \;\Bigg|\;s_t,a_t \Bigg]\Bigg] \quad\text{(lemma 1)}\\ &= \sum_{t=1}^{H}\mathbb E_{s_t,a_t}\left[ \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t) \mathbb E_\pi\left[ \sum_{t'=t}^{H}\gamma^{t'-1}r_{t'} \;\middle|\;s_t,a_t \right]\right]. \quad\text{(lemma 3)}\\ &= \sum_{t=1}^{H}\mathbb E_\pi\left[ \gamma^{t-1} \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t) Q_t^{\pi_\theta}(s_t,a_t) \right] \\ &= \sum_{t=1}^{H}\mathbb E_\pi\left[ \gamma^{t-1} \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t) \left(Q_t^{\pi_\theta}(s_t,a_t)-b(s_t)\right) \right]. \quad\text{(baseline function)} \end{aligned}∇θ​J(πθ​)​=t=1∑H​Eπ​[∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​)t′=t∑H​γt′−1rt′​]=t=1∑H​Est​,at​​[Eπ​[∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​)t′=t∑H​γt′−1rt′​​st​,at​]](lemma 1)=t=1∑H​Est​,at​​[∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​)Eπ​[t′=t∑H​γt′−1rt′​​st​,at​]].(lemma 3)=t=1∑H​Eπ​[γt−1∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​)Qtπθ​​(st​,at​)]=t=1∑H​Eπ​[γt−1∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​)(Qtπθ​​(st​,at​)−b(st​))].(baseline function)​

If we learn a value function for b(st)b(s_t)b(st​), define the advantage function

Aπ(st,at)=Qtπθ(st,at)−Vπ(st).A^\pi(s_t,a_t) = Q_t^{\pi_\theta}(s_t,a_t)-V^\pi(s_t).Aπ(st​,at​)=Qtπθ​​(st​,at​)−Vπ(st​).

Thus, we have

∇θJ(πθ)=∑t=1HEπ[γt−1∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st)Aπ(st,at)]≃∑t=1HEπ[∇θlog⁡πθ(at∣st)Aπ(st,at)].\begin{aligned} \nabla_\theta J(\pi_\theta) &= \sum_{t=1}^{H}\mathbb E_\pi\left[ \gamma^{t-1} \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t) A^\pi(s_t,a_t) \right] \\ &\simeq \sum_{t=1}^{H}\mathbb E_\pi\left[ \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t) A^\pi(s_t,a_t) \right]. \end{aligned}∇θ​J(πθ​)​=t=1∑H​Eπ​[γt−1∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​)Aπ(st​,at​)]≃t=1∑H​Eπ​[∇θ​logπθ​(at​∣st​)Aπ(st​,at​)].​

In practice we often omit the γt−1\gamma^{t-1}γt−1 as it gives less and less weight to later steps over a long horizon.
